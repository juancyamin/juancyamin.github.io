\section{Job Market Paper}
\papertitle{https://juancyamin.github.io/files/poverty-targeting-with-imperfect-information.pdf}{Poverty Targeting with Imperfect Information}
I study how antipoverty programs should allocate transfers when income is known only through noisy predictions. I show that the standard plug-in rule is inadmissible. I then develop a nonparametric empirical Bayes rule whose Bayes regret is bounded by the mean squared error in estimating oracle posterior mean poverty gaps. In simulations using surveys from nine African countries, the rule reaches about 1.8 times as many poor people as plug-in OLS with the same budget and achieves the same average poverty-gap reduction with 6.7 percent less spending.
\paperend

\section{Other Working Papers}
\papertitle{https://juancyamin.github.io/files/when-and-how-to-pilot.pdf}{When and How to Pilot: Design Rules for Two-Wave Experiments}
I propose a Conditional Minimax Regret (CMR) rule for treatment assignment in two-wave experiments. CMR minimizes worst-case regret over a finite-sample confidence set for treatment and control variances. It retains the worst-case protection of balanced assignment with high probability, converges to the Neyman allocation as the pilot grows, and attains the minimax-regret rate up to constants. In simulations calibrated to four field experiments, it avoids the severe small-pilot losses of feasible Neyman allocation while matching its large-pilot gains.
\paperend

\needspace{5\baselineskip}
\textbf{Two-Way Effects Models: A Nonparametric Empirical Bayes Approach}\par
with Cole Davis \hfill \textit{Draft available upon request}\par\vspace{0.13em}
We develop nonparametric empirical Bayes shrinkage estimators for AKM worker and firm effects. We also estimate how the mean and dispersion of worker effects vary with firm wage premia, accounting for correlated estimation errors.
\paperend

\section{Publications}
\papertitle{https://juancyamin.github.io/files/subnational-alignment-corruption.pdf}{Birds of a Feather Collude Together: Subnational Alignment and Corruption}
with Leopoldo Fergusson, Arturo Harker, and Carlos Molina\par
\textit{Forthcoming in the American Political Science Review}\par\vspace{0.13em}
Using a regression discontinuity design in Colombia, we find that partisan alignment between mayors and governors increases the share of ``ghost'' students by about 0.3 standard deviations, inflating education transfers without improving genuine enrollment or student performance. Alignment also increases patronage and electoral fraud risk, while aligned politicians enjoy better future electoral prospects.
